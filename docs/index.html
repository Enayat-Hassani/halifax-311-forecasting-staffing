52-week backtest · Erlang A · half-hourly

Similar forecast error, different staffing risk

Does the most accurate forecast also give the best staffing? This page tests that question on public call data from Halifax 311, the city’s contact centre. Nine forecast runs were compared over 52 weeks, by their error and by the staffing they lead to.

  1. 1 · ForecastEach Monday, nine runs forecast calls for the week starting seven days later. Capacity forecasts extend 13 and 26 weeks ahead.
  2. 2 · StaffFor the one-week horizon, Erlang A turns calls into the agents needed in each half-hour. Capacity forecasts become FTE estimates.
  3. 3 · ScoreWe compare staffing error and cost. A short hour can cost more than a spare one.

Before modelling, the published data was repaired: one year was largely counted twice (about 61% too high), and later timestamps were four hours off.

Accuracy ranks the models. Bias sets the risk.

WAPE and staffing error rank the nine runs almost the same way. But two runs with the same error can miss in different directions. A forecast that runs low leaves more half-hours short, and being short usually costs more than having a spare agent.

How the adjustment works

For each model, actual calls are divided by forecast calls in every open half-hour of 26 training weeks. This shows how the model usually misses. The cost ratio r sets how cautious to be: the target is the quantile q = r / (1 + r), so 4× gives q = 0.80. The forecast is multiplied by that quantile, and staffing is scored on the test year.

Capacity horizon

Months ahead, the model matters less than the buffer

Capacity plans are set 13 to 26 weeks out, in weekly totals rather than half-hours. At that level, weekly error stays near 9–12% for every model, even 26 weeks ahead. In FTE at 4×, the same adjustment lowers every model’s weekly cost, and the gap between models falls to under half an FTE.

What this means for planning

  1. Judge forecasts by staffing, not WAPE alone. When costs are equal, the two agree (rank correlation 0.97–1.00). When a short hour costs more, the direction of the error changes the ranking: at 16×, Chronos-2 falls from third to eighth of nine.
  2. Check which way a model misses. A forecast that runs 4.4% low reaches a modelled service level of only 78.4%, below the 80% target, with the same total error as a model that meets it.
  3. Adjust the forecast before switching models. One cost-based adjustment cut the cost by 15–31% at 4×. For capacity plans 13 weeks out, runs differ by less than one FTE per week after it.

Scope
Scheduling results estimate net staffing for one queue and one test year. They do not model shrinkage, shifts or rosters. Capacity FTE uses assumed occupancy and shrinkage, but excludes interval safety staffing and roster constraints. Neither analysis reflects HRM staffing or actual costs. Full assumptions ↗
